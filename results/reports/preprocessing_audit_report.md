## 3. Issues, Uncertainties & Methodological Findings

### Issue 1: Terminology & Signal Processing Misnomer in Figure 10 (ERP vs. ERD)
* **Severity:** **Important** (Scientific interpretation & reporting)
* **Evidence:** In `batch_preprocess_pipeline.py` (lines 395–440), Figure 10 is generated by computing the direct arithmetic mean of raw voltage values across trials:
  $$\text{Mean Waveform}(t) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N X_i(t)$$
* **Scientific Analysis:**
  * In cognitive neuroscience, time-domain voltage averaging isolates **Event-Related Potentials (ERPs)** and **Movement-Related Cortical Potentials (MRCPs)**—phase-locked evoked voltage deflections.
  * **Event-Related Desynchronization (ERD)** is an *induced* (non-phase-locked) drop in oscillatory power in the Mu ($8–12\text{ Hz}$) and Beta ($13–30\text{ Hz}$) bands. Because oscillatory phase varies randomly from trial to trial, averaging raw microvolt waveforms across 431 trials causes the sinusoidal peaks and troughs to cancel out toward $0.0\ \mu\text{V}$ (as seen in Figure 10 where the mean waveform hovers within $\pm 2\ \mu\text{V}$).
  * Figure 10 demonstrates the time-domain grand-average voltage waveform (ERP), but **it does not display ERD power drop**. Claiming Figure 10 demonstrates "clear ERD dynamics" is a methodological misnomer.
* **Recommended Next Action:**
  * Update figure titles and documentation to label Figure 10 accurately as: **"Grand-Average Filtered Voltage Waveforms (ERPs)"**.
  * When ERD analysis is conducted in future phases, compute instantaneous power ($V^2$) or Morlet wavelet energy *prior* to cross-trial averaging, expressing power as percentage change relative to pre-cue rest ($T_0$).

### Issue 2: Peak-to-Peak Amplitude Calculation Discrepancy (63 vs. 66 Trials)
* **Severity:** **Minor** (Documentation consistency)
* **Evidence:**
  * In `preprocessing_pipeline.py` and `batch_preprocess_pipeline.py`:
    $$\text{Max Ch PtP} = \max(\text{PtP}_{\text{C3}}, \text{PtP}_{\text{Cz}}, \text{PtP}_{\text{C4}})$$
    This formula evaluates each electrode channel independently, identifying **63 trials** with $\ge 200\ \mu\text{V}$.
  * In `validate_consolidated_dataset.py` (line 91):
    $$\text{Global PtP} = \max(X_i) - \min(X_i) \quad \text{across all 3 channels combined}$$
    This formula identifies **66 trials** with $\ge 200\ \mu\text{V}$.
* **Scientific Analysis:**
  * The 3 discrepant trials have individual electrode ranges $< 200\ \mu\text{V}$ (e.g. C3 spans $[-90, +95]\ \mu\text{V}$ and C4 spans $[-95, +105]\ \mu\text{V}$), but because C4 reaches $+105\ \mu\text{V}$ while C3 dips to $-95\ \mu\text{V}$, their cross-channel span is $200\ \mu\text{V}$.
  * In EEG quality control, peak-to-peak thresholds are conventionally evaluated **per electrode channel**, making the 63-trial figure the standard metric.
* **Recommended Next Action:**
  * Standardize the documentation and validation script to report the per-channel maximum PtP (63 trials), explicitly explaining the cross-channel difference (66 trials).

### Issue 3: Concentration of High-Amplitude Trials in Specific Participants
* **Severity:** **Important** (Machine learning robustness & sensitivity analysis)
* **Evidence:** The 63 trials with per-channel PtP $> 200\ \mu\text{V}$ are heavily clustered in three participants:
  * `S015`: **28 trials** (out of 45, 62.2% of the participant's data)
  * `S017`: **16 trials** (out of 45, 35.6%)
  * `S013`: **9 trials** (out of 45, 20.0%)
  * `S003`: 4 trials
  * `S009`: 2 trials
  * `S010`: 2 trials
  * `S007`: 1 trial
  * `S014`: 1 trial
  * **Remaining 11 participants:** **0 trials $> 200\ \mu\text{V}$**
  * Maximum observed trial: Trial 557 (`S013R08`, Class 0) reaches $490.28\ \mu\text{V}$ peak-to-peak on C3.
* **Scientific Analysis:**
  * Participant `S015` consistently displays high amplitudes across almost all trials, indicating higher individual physiological baseline voltage, thinner skull anatomy, or different amplifier calibration rather than isolated transient artifacts.
  * However, individual extreme trials (such as $490.28\ \mu\text{V}$ in `S013`) are consistent with ocular blinks or transient muscle twitches (EMG leakage).
  * Because linear classifiers (e.g., LDA, Logistic Regression) and spatial covariance estimators (e.g., Common Spatial Patterns / CSP) can be sensitive to high-amplitude outliers, unnormalized trials could distort model weights.
* **Recommended Next Action:**
  * Retaining these trials for initial baseline training is defensible under non-destructive screening.
  * **Crucial recommendation for model training:** Implement per-subject standardization (z-score normalization per channel per participant) or robust covariance estimation.
  * Conduct a **sensitivity analysis**: evaluate model performance on the full dataset (853 trials) versus an artifact-cleaned subset (excluding trials with $\text{PtP} > 250\ \mu\text{V}$).

### Issue 4: Epoch Window Dynamics (Reaction Time & VEP vs. Sustained Imagery)
* **Severity:** **Minor / Analytical Option** (Feature engineering consideration)
* **Evidence:** Epochs are extracted from $t = 0.0\text{ s}$ (cue appearance) to $t = 4.0\text{ s}$ ($640\text{ samples}$).
* **Scientific Analysis:**
  * When a visual prompt appears at $t = 0.0\text{ s}$, human cognitive latency (reaction time) delays the onset of active motor rehearsal by $200–500\text{ ms}$. Furthermore, the initial $100–300\text{ ms}$ contains visual evoked potentials (P100, N200) elicited by the monitor prompt.
  * In BCI literature using EEGMMIDB, several studies analyze sub-windows such as $[0.5\text{ s}, 3.5\text{ s}]$ ($3.0\text{ s}$, 480 samples) or $[1.0\text{ s}, 4.0\text{ s}]$ to capture stable, sustained sensorimotor rhythm desynchronization.
  * **However, our current $[0.0\text{ s}, 4.0\text{ s}]$ extraction is completely defensible and superior for data retention:** because the full $4.0\text{ s}$ tensor is preserved, any downstream feature extractor or model can slice `[:, :, 80:560]` in memory without modifying the underlying dataset files.
* **Recommended Next Action:**
  * Retain the current $[0.0\text{ s}, 4.0\text{ s}]$ dataset intact.
  * In the upcoming feature extraction and modeling phase, test both full-window ($0.0–4.0\text{ s}$) and reaction-time-trimmed ($0.5–3.5\text{ s}$) representations to determine empirical classification impact.

---

### Issue 5: Scope of Quality Screening Checks
* **Severity:** **Minor** (Scope clarification)
* **Evidence:** In `preprocessing_pipeline.py`, quality checks screen for:
  1. Non-finite values (`np.isnan`, `np.isinf`)
  2. Flatline channels (`np.std < 1e-4`)
  3. Overlap with all-channel zero acquisition pauses
* **Scientific Analysis:**
  * These checks guarantee **numerical validity and absence of catastrophic recording failure**.
  * They do not perform biological artifact cleaning (e.g., Independent Component Analysis for blink removal, automated threshold rejection, or muscle EMG filtering).
* **Recommended Next Action:**
  * Explicitly document in the project methodology that the preprocessing stage performs *signal quality and numerical screening*, not *artifact removal/denoising*.
  * Rely on bandpass filtering ($8–30\text{ Hz}$) and robust spatial filters (CSP) to suppress out-of-band artifacts during modeling.
